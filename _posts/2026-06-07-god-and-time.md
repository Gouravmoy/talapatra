Of late, I have been fascinated by slow processes. Systems and processes that take days, weeks, or months to unfold. There are many processes around us that are slow.. An example would be almost any natural process.

I recently built a terrarium in my home. Once the setup was complete, I placed it beside my stairs in a well-lit area where it received light from a skylight above. After a few days I saw that the glass of the terrarium became foggy with condensation. This happened because it was a closed terrarium as opposed to an open terrarium where water vapor has a way of escaping.

It took a few days for the water vapor to condense on the glass. By the third day, the glass was almost completely obscured with watery mist on the inside surface. Then on day four, the condensation on the glass inside the terrarium had become oversaturated and had started coalescing together as small rivulets of water. These rivulets were very thin and travelled the path of least resistance in a zig zag manner down the glass and back to the soil.

I was fascinated with this slow process, the inevitability of it. In school, I had learned about the water cycle - how water gets evaporated from various water bodies, rises into the atmosphere where it cools down and falls back as rain. I knew this always from a theoretical point of view but never imagined I would be able to see this process in a small terrarium ecosystem

Natural processes are slow - sometime unimaginably so. In natural processes, there is never a sense of hurriedness or the need for instant gratification. Nothing is rushed. Things take their own time and proceed at their own pace. They can sometimes speed up, for example rain and thunderstorms or like the short time it took for the rivulets to cover the final few inches to reach the soil. But they are always slow to build up.

My day job is mostly busy and reactive. There is a constant need to respond to events in near real-time. And that is why this slowness fascinates me. It makes me wonder about other slow processes. Like a tree growing over years, becoming tall, sprouting leaves, shedding them in winter, and regenerating them in spring. Animals in the wild that grow and live for many years. What about extremely slow processes like the formation of mountains, rivers and evolution. Process that take thousands and sometimes millions of years, but happen a little every moment.

Makes me wonder if there is an entity out there for whom a solar year would be the blink of an eyelid. Our world would be a close terrarium through which they would occasionally peer, curious about what had changed. For them, human lives would be fleeting things. They wouldn't concern themselves with every human life, just as one cannot pay attention to every blade of grass inside a terrarium. Maybe they, like me, would one day be sitting and observing through the glass, wondering about time.
